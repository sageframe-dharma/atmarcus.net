Teaching

Andrew Todd Marcus

Design educator · 25 years teaching design and building the systems that let others teach it

I have taught design for twenty-five years, to first-year architecture students and thesis candidates, to middle and high school students at NuVu, and to the teachers who run programs of their own. The studios here represent four approaches. Design learning systematized as an intuitive unfolding, so that non-designers arrive at the iterative process and at critical thought. Collaboration with subject experts far from design, so that the design process can be turned on emerging technologies. Complex ideas from critical theory put in front of young people. And a long-standing interest in accessible and empathic design, from the conceptual to the built: design as advocacy.

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Fall 2018 · prototype introductory design course · 10 days · designed with James Addison · I conceived of, designed, prepared, and oversaw the execution

On NuVuStudio →Subtle Individuality →Game of Kings →Pawns of the Government →Contemporary Bias →Partisan Fracture →

A studio for students who could already fabricate but had never been taught to design. NuVu's long-term students arrived proficient in process and tools; this prototype gave them the foundations. It was built as a model studio for NuVuX partner programs and as a training studio for curriculum designers, and it became a core studio, adapted since for students and for teachers. Seven exercises, each a day or two, each answering a piece of three questions: What is design? How do we design? How does design shape our world?

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Spring 2019 · model module-based studio · 10 days · co-coached with Nadine Zaza

On NuVuStudio →The Smell Step →Sunrise Symphony →

A studio built to test a new curriculum model: discrete learning modules that could be recombined as NuVu grew. It became the template for the studios that followed. The subject was the senses. Students asked how the layering of sensory exposure defines experience and memory, and how sensory information triggers bodily response, and worked through a progressive, data-driven sequence to design devices and installations that let a user experience the world through a sensory connection they had never felt. Unlike most NuVu studios, the final product was a proof of concept rather than a working prototype; the sequence was the point.

I
5-Sense Theory

Using five-sense graphs, students recorded experiences, plotted them, and compared the data.

the exercise on NuVu →
II
Sense Maps

With a smell-walk mapping kit, students refocused their reading of Central Square on one sense and learned to map data with a privileged intention.

the exercise on NuVu →
III
Experiencing Memory

Students experienced Ragnar Kjartansson's The Visitors at the ICA Boston, to learn how spatial sense can be engaged through the other senses, and how a work can obliquely trigger individual and shared emotion.

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Fall 2019 – December 2021 · five studios · co-coached with Ammar Ahmed · client the City of Cambridge, funded by a Cambridge Arts grant · construction documents and fabrication Ethan Lacy with Formateria · playground design Weston & Sampson

On NuVuStudio I →Studio II →Studio III →Pipe Dreams →

A multi-year studio series I conceived to produce a full-scale installation for the Louis A. DePasquale Universal Design Playground at Danehy Park, commissioned by the City of Cambridge. Over five studios, middle and high school students moved through the whole arc of an architectural project: conceptual abstraction exercises, sketch models, diagrams, presentation drawings, fabrication documents, budgets. They learned parametric modeling and the coordination a public commission demands, with city officials, landscape architects, engineers, fabricators, and disability advisors, and across the series they became one design team. They took part in construction administration and helped carry the project through installation.

The result is Pipe Dreams, an art-play sculpture that takes the park's history as a landfill and gives it back as an eroded storm pipe: a tunnel of curved wooden planks with window openings and integrated handholds, on the playground that Boston Magazine named Best Outdoor Play Space in 2024.

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Spring 2017 · 15 days · co-coached with Lee Cusack and Jenny Kinaird · film by Amro Arida

On NuVuStudio →Breaking Bread →

Cooking, eating, and sharing food is a basic biological and social act. In this studio students worked with and for Lee Cusack, a UX designer with spastic quadriplegic cerebral palsy, to understand what the most basic human need asks of someone with SQCP, and to design systems and devices for it. They were led through exercises in empathy, human-centred design, and contemporary performance art, and the studio ended as a performance: Breaking Bread, a ritualized sharing of bread built on intimate interactions, with small teams responsible for the food system, the central watering hole, the food-delivery beak, and the running of the performance itself.

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Winter 2017 · 11 days · co-coached with Katia Zolotovsky, bio-designer and architect · lab at BosLab

On NuVuStudio →NutriSensor →Algae Shade →

An experimental studio that took NuVu's curriculum into biology, and asked how a technology like CRISPR could be handled in a conceptual studio. It was the first NuVu studio with a rigorous lab component, and it opened the way to later work in ecosystem design, living architecture, and genetics.

Bacteria in the body outnumber human cells ten to one. If biology can engineer those organisms for new functions, what new abilities could we have? Students began with a crash course in cell and molecular biology and genetics and ran a biosensor and genetics lab at BosLab, a cooperative laboratory. Then they imagined, designed, and built wearable bioreactors and proofs of concept: devices that house engineered living cells and work in symbiosis with the wearer.

NuVu Studio, Cambridge · Fall 2017 · 10 days · co-coached with Seth Alter

On NuVuStudio →Ant Farm →Gravity Party →

Every game occupies its own spatial universe, and an architecture that governs play and sets the depth of the player's experience. How are those worlds imagined and made? Students began with game and architectural precedents and with critical reading of both, then used the tools of spatial description from architecture to conceive, physically develop, and digitally build a game in Unity with clear spatial ideas. Form-building exercises and orthographic and isometric drawings came first, then physical puzzles of spatial complexity, then the games. Two projects.

 
Ant Farm

A student. A worker ant carries a strawberry to its hungry queen through a tunnel world designed to make the player notice the spaces they move through and feel differently in each.

 
Gravity Party

A student. A spaceship whose gravity generator is failing; the player crosses it as gravity flips, and has to see the environment anew each time.

platform

Citizen Schools · Fall 2023 · prototype program, 15 weeks · I designed the module and built the platform

On NuVuThe platform →

A prototype program to put the new Citizen Schools Learning Framework into practice, training our own team and a first cohort of partner educators in the experience-based learning pedagogy and curriculum. I bootstrapped a shared, studio-based learning platform from off-the-shelf tools on a budget of $250, to show what a collaborative learning space could be; it ran synchronous and asynchronous sessions, peer learning, and community engagement. Module 1 gave educators the creative process from the inside: they developed projects of their own, collaborated, and brought components into their classrooms, and came out ready to run authentic experience-based learning with external mentors and activated communities.

Robot Tro(U)(o)Pe

NuVu, Winter 2019
Studio →

What is a robot, and what is a uniquely robotic personality? Students began with animated and mechanical caricatures, then read about and built Braitenberg vehicles, hypothetical self-operating machines with increasingly intricate and not always civilized behaviour. Through gestural mechanics they gave each robot a personality, introduced the robots to one another in short films in the manner of reality television, and fitted them with sensors and actuators to respond to the robots they liked and disliked. The result was a surrealist troupe improvising its personalities in public.

Memory Vessels

NuVu, Winter 2019
Studio →Box of Memories →

Memory, as narrative and sense experience, forms identity. Students first looked inward, abstracting their own stories to engage the senses, then worked with residents of Mt. Pleasant Home, an eldercare facility in Jamaica Plain, helping them express and record their stories, and built memory vessels to carry the depth and lived experience of those lives.

Kinetic Prosthetics

NuVu, Spring 2017 (two studios)
Studio →O.C.D Vice →

Prosthetics as extensions of the self: how mechanical additions to the body change what we can do and how we present ourselves. Students identified a user, worked from that person's challenges and needs, and designed and built a kinetic prosthesis to extend ability or communication.

Adaptive Myo Devices

NuVu, Spring 2016 · part of the NuVu Lab for Assistive Technology
Studio →Gestural Light Board →

Adaptive design gives people with disabilities the custom adaptations they need for their developmental, social, academic, and vocational lives. Students built assistive hardware that responds to the body's movement through the Myo armband, whose EMG sensors read the forearm's muscles for gesture, motion, orientation, and rotation: electronics and Arduino, 3D modeling and fabrication, and open-source designs for others to use.

Denatured Design

NuVu, 2017 (Fall and Winter)
Studio →Directional Hearing →

An introductory studio for middle school students with no design or technical experience, on how we experience the world and the biases in that experience. Students made maps and field recordings, read and built conceptual worlds from Calvino's Invisible Cities, and made tools and devices with which to map the world around them, learning rapid prototyping, programming, and basic electronics along the way. Wearables for performers, devices for artists and scientists, tools for everyday people.

Waking the City

NuVu, Spring 2018
Studio →Dress to Express →

How the city forms the imaginations of its inhabitants, and how their imaginations form the city. Students learned mechanical design, fabrication, electronics, and Arduino to build urban interventions, wearables, autonomous objects, enabling devices, that surprise, delight, and wake the city's inhabitants to what they no longer see.

Architecture After the Animal

NuVu, Spring 2019
Studio →The Compost-Chute →

Students hypothesized how an urban animal, now an external agent, could shape the way we build, and become part of the conversation. How do we design a window, a door, a wall to make affordances for other bodies? Taking the parallax view in which animal life plays a part, the studio expanded the vocabulary of architecture.

Adaptive Mechatronics

NuVu, Fall 2017 · with Access Sport America and Ross Lilley
Studio →Assistive Skater →

Traumatic brain injury contributes each year to a substantial number of deaths and permanent disabilities. Through Access Sport America, students worked with athletes living with brain injury, stroke, and other conditions, and fabricated enabling, non-traditional wearable mechanical and mechatronic devices, each designed for one person, so that they could take part again in an activity or experience.

Biofashion

NuVu at Kelvinside, Summer 2017
Studio →Living Dress →

Nature is full of form, texture, and colour, from sea caterpillars to redwoods. Using biology as inspiration and digital design and rapid prototyping as the means, students made high-tech fashion for dancers, performers, musicians, and everyday people, with unusual materials, complex patterns, and robotics in the final pieces.

Reinvisioning Traditional Games: Brazil

NuVu, Winter 2016 · with Tencent and UNESCO
Studio →Capoeira →

With the gamemaker Tencent, which has digitized traditional and folk games collected with UNESCO for the public domain, students studied what traditional games imply about the world-view of the civilizations that made them, and how those civilizations used games to teach, then transformed traditional Brazilian games for a younger generation with current technology and pedagogy.

P.E.T. Patient Emotion Therapy

NuVu, Winter 2016
Studio →Humidifier Flower Light →

Fear and stress are linked to poorer outcomes for patients in surgery, procedures, clinic visits, and hospital stays. Students followed a 2D-to-3D iterative process to learn how to build emotion and feeling into objects, then made a functional companion device for an acute visit or a long hospital stay, an object that turns the patient's state toward calm through interaction.

Easing Cerebral Palsy

NuVu, Winter 2014 (two studios) · with Instituto Nuevo Amanecer, Monterrey, and Javier Leal of Más Libertad Menos Barreras
Studio →Arte Para Todos →

Students worked remotely with Instituto Nuevo Amanecer to create devices and wearables for children with cerebral palsy: two two-week studios full-time at NuVu, then a week in Monterrey working with the children directly, joined by students from Monterrey Tec. Three of the projects won Core77 Design Awards in the student category. My role was conceptual development for the studio and design consultation on the final projects.
